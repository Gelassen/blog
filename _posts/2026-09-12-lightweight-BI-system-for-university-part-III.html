Executive summary

Within a single intensive week, with AI used as a force-multiplier, we moved a production educational analytics product from a flat, tab-heavy interface to a layered decision-support workspace. The work covered the full cycle: market and trend research, a timed key-user scenario, functional information architecture, a formal coverage matrix (every requirement still has an address), stakeholder confirmation, visual direction, and migration of the working product onto the new UX.

The original baseline came from direct work in Moodle: a teacher’s group performance review took roughly 40 minutes of navigating the LMS gradebook, reports and individual activity logs. The product goal has always been to bring that down to 5–10 minutes with explainable analytics. Bloomberg-style density remains the cautionary example — immensely capable, yet frequently rejected by anyone who is not a full-time power user. The redesign makes sophisticated, explainable analytics feel approachable and productive rather than something users quietly avoid.

This post is the design companion to the earlier technical write-ups: Lightweight BI system for university, part I and part II (how the analytics is built) . Those posts describe the data model, the rule-based engine and the nine-tab Streamlit surface. Here we focus on why and how the interaction model itself changed.

Where UX of data-heavy apps actually went

Before redesigning anything we re-checked the real trajectory of professional interfaces over the last decade, not the marketing slogans:

  • Content before chrome. The first decisive move was rejecting heavy decorative frames and pure “chrome.” The principle is simple: the data and the task come first; the surrounding interface must recede.
  • Material Design and systemisation. Material (and parallel systems) brought shared structure — cards, elevation, consistent components, clearer hierarchy. Predictability improved; so did the temptation to over-decorate.
  • Less fancy Material, still more focused on data. The subsequent wave dialled the theatre back — fewer shadows, quieter colour, less motion — while doubling down on data legibility, progressive disclosure and task efficiency. The interface became quieter so that the information could speak louder.

For educational analytics the implication is direct: teachers do not need another terminal. They need a calm, hierarchical surface that answers “what needs attention this week?” in seconds, then supports deeper exploration without forcing a complete mental re-model of the tool.

The old design (what we started from)

The first production surface was a Streamlit dashboard with a global sidebar and nine specialised tabs:

  • Themes (level-1 “flower” overview)
  • Questions
  • Student
  • Insights
  • Risk group
  • Trends
  • Score structure
  • Deadlines / attempts / effort
  • Attendance and activity

It already delivered the analytical model: rule-based findings with evidence, hypothesis, alternatives, recommendations and limits; theme map as orientation anchor; full coverage of the ТЗ queries.

Strengths we kept: complete analytical coverage, explainable (non-LLM) findings, solid incremental engine, theme map as the central orientation device.

Pain points that still stood in the way of the 5–10 minute target:

  • Flat information architecture. Nine equally weighted tabs forced constant context reconstruction.
  • Cognitive overload. Dense tables and uniform visual weight made the tool feel closer to a terminal than to decision support.
  • Weak progressive disclosure. Findings and full BI lived on the same mental plane; the weekly path had no privileged short route.
  • Silent context-loss risk. Moving between tabs could feel like starting over; filters lived in a sidebar that was easy to forget.

The ~40-minute baseline itself was measured in direct work with Moodle (gradebook, reports, activity logs), not on this first dashboard. The Streamlit surface was already an attempt to shrink that time; the UX redesign removes the remaining friction that kept the real path longer than the target.

Key user scenario broken down by timing

The primary job is a weekly (or post-control) performance review. The original cost was measured in Moodle; the first product version reduced part of that cost; the redesign targets the remaining friction so the full path lands in the 5–10 minute band.

Baseline — direct work in Moodle (~40 minutes)

  1. 0–10 min. Open course, gradebook, multiple reports; locate the relevant group and period.
  2. 10–25 min. Cross-check topic results, individual attempts, attendance and activity logs; open student by student where needed.
  3. 25–40 min. Mentally synthesise what needs attention (which topics, which students, which items to simplify or repeat) and form an action list.

First product version — still longer than the target

Nine flat tabs + sidebar filters already replaced raw Moodle navigation with structured panels and explainable findings. Orientation and cross-referencing, however, still cost time: the user had to reconstruct “what is still relevant for this slice” after every tab switch.

After redesign — designed 5–10 minute path

  1. 0–30 sec. Global context already shows the active slice (course › theme › student › work type). Three KPI cards + theme map answer “what is the state of the group?”
  2. 30 sec – 3 min. Switch to Problems (Findings). Prioritised signals with severity, short rationale and “Подробнее”. No need to hunt across tabs.
  3. 3–7 min. Open one or two findings into full xAI cards (hypothesis, evidence, alternatives, recommendations, limits). Drill into theme or student while the global context stays intact.
  4. 7–10 min. If a specific indicator is required, jump to Data → the matching panel. Every ТЗ query still has an address; the default path no longer forces visiting all of them.

The time saving is not “fewer screens.” It is the removal of repeated re-orientation and the separation of three jobs that used to compete on the same flat surface: orient → attend to problems → fetch a specific number.

Functional design & information architecture

The redesign does not delete capability. It re-sequences it. The new contract is:

  • Global context (course › theme › topic › student + work type) is always visible and never silently reset.
  • Three layers of work on the same slice: Overview (orient) → Problems / Findings (attend) → Data / Analytics (fetch any indicator).
  • Theme map (“цветочки”) remains the central orientation device on Overview.
  • Every ТЗ query 1–14 has an explicit address under Data (Performance / Learning process / Assessment / Students).

UX contract in short: Overview orients; Findings attract attention; Data is the full address book of indicators. Search is secondary to hierarchical context.

Coverage matrix — requirements ↔ new UX

A formal matrix was produced so stakeholders could verify that a navigation change does not equal functional loss. Two independent acceptance criteria were kept separate:

  • UX coverage — is the 30-second / 2-minute / 5–10-minute path comfortable?
  • Requirement coverage — where does each US / ТЗ item live physically?

Excerpt (US → UX location in IA v3):

Requirement UX location (v3) Coverage
US-1.1 Overview by themes Overview → Theme map yes
US-1.4 Short interpretation Problems (Findings); optional signal under map yes
US-2.1–2.6 Queries 1–14 Data → Performance / Learning / Assessment / Students yes
US-3.1–3.4 Context continuity Global context bar + “never silent reset” contract yes
US-4.1–4.3 Student / Risk Data → Students / Risk · Student card yes
US-5.1–5.2 XAI / patterns Problems → detail → Finding + Evidence + Action yes
ТЗ §3.1–3.2 Interpretation & risk Problems · Difficulty · Risk yes

Current → Proposed mapping of the nine Streamlit surfaces was also documented (themes → Overview flowers; insights → Problems; remaining tabs → Data groups; sidebar → global context). Message to stakeholders: “navigation changed, requirements not lost.”

Applying the final design — current product

Implementation moved from the functional concept and matrix to a production React workspace in the same intensive cycle. Visible outcomes:

  • Calm, modern visual system — content before chrome, without early-Material decoration.
  • Persistent global context + three clear layers (Overview / Problems / Data).
  • Theme map retained as the orientation anchor; Findings list as the attention layer; full indicator catalogue under Data.
  • xAI cards with hypothesis, evidence, alternatives, recommendations and limits remain first-class.
  • Core weekly review path designed for 5–10 minutes (from the Moodle baseline of ~40 minutes).

Value delivered

The product is no longer a dense analytical terminal that users must learn to tolerate. It is a decision-support environment that:

  • Respects limited attention — orient, attend, then fetch.
  • Keeps every requirement reachable (the coverage matrix is the proof).
  • Builds trust through transparent, rule-based xAI cards.
  • Preserves analytical depth while making the default path short.
  • Brings the weekly review from the Moodle baseline of ~40 minutes into a focused 5–10 minute decision loop.

The whole cycle — trend research, timed scenario, functional IA, coverage matrix, stakeholder alignment, visual design and functional migration — was executed in one intensive week with AI as a core productivity partner. That combination of depth and speed is the skill the work demonstrates.
