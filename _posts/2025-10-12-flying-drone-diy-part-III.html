Intro

This publication describes assembling and tuning drone which design and configuration has been covered in the previous publications.

The process is quite straightforward, but still sophisticated -- you need to have domain specific knowledge to get this done.
ArduPilot team made a good job to describe this process, but there are still a room to bridge the gaps.

Hardware part

Soldering connectors

ESCs and motors comes without soldered connectors, so you have to solder them by yourself.
Due soldering I faced with issues to bold the solder and metall of the connectors. By using colophony and several attempts to couple solder and metall I achieved satsfactory results.
My skills in soldering are basic, may be due research you will find more superior techniques.

Soldering PDB and cope with EMI

The next phase is soldering ESCs to power distribution board. There are similar issues with soldering to PDB which increasing demand in accuracy to avoid overheating the circuit and burnout traces and elements around.
Another issue was to adjust ESC cables to the PDB. Chosen ESC supports 80 Ampers of current and used quite bold cables (12 awg). The choosen PDB in this way should support at least 320 A (sic!) of current and it does (at least by specification)! However, places\holes to solder wires are twice as less than neccessary. Adjusting diameter of the cables or finding right extra connectors are solution for it.

In all cases I saw the PDB has open structure and open surface. Keeping in mind how much current power comes through this it is important to protect it from accidental touch as well as from unexpected rain.

Another issue is EMI. Current which goes over wires creates a electromagnetic field around itself which affect the current (and signal!) in another wires around. Higher the current - the higher the impact. In this way it is crucial to put far away from each other a battery, PDB, flight controller and RC receiver.
Aluminum foil could give extra protection from EMI, especially on the high fequency current. In my case I used for layer between FC and PDB.

Engines: CV vs CCV

If you use ArduPilot, then you should pay extra attention to motors ordering. ArduPilot expects motors ordering on the frame and on the each frame type it would be its own ordering.

The order of motors depends on the direction in which engine works - clockwise (CW) and counter clockwise (CCW). It was done to stabilize the drone movement, otherwise the torque of the working engines will force copter to move over its circle. To cope with it CV and CVV motors are setup by pair - one pair on a single axis, - and goes one-by-one.
The direction in which motor spinning around could be changed by interchaning any two from three cables which goes to ESC.

Not only engines ordering, but also propellers ordering matters. Props are made with one and opposite directions in which they are screwed to grasp the air. By observing it you will easily understand in which direction they should move to effectively create a thurst. After launch it neccessary to check in which direction motor moves and does it have an appropriate prop!

Pixhawk board has a set of pins targeted to connect with ESCs. MAIN OUT panel has numbered order. Reference documentation menitioned above to figure out the right number for each engine.

Flight controller: setup RC

Radio receiver also came disassembled. Using documentation you have to figure the right pins for your configuration and solder right wires.

I have to order a new cable to solder it and meet with Pixhawk connectors. Pixhawk supports JST-GH connectors (6 pins, 2mm) and and you have to pay extra attention to *exact* name, because under JST prefix there are sveral models on the market.

Another interesting point to be aware of is wires coloring. Beccause JST-GH is standart you could expect the unified color ordering, however in this case it was not -- black doesn't mean GROUND and red doesn't mean POWER. Alawys refer to documentation and pins descriptions there, otherwise you could damage your circuit.

The final thing is pin ordering. Pixhawk text labels were somewhat misleading -- I mixed up the 1st pin for 6th and vice versa. More experience with JST-GH and plain mulitemeter check should secure you from this pitfall.

Mitigate EMI. The primary source of EMI is the battery itself, key power lines - PCB and ESCs - and radio reciver. FC should be positioned as far as possible from each of them to avoid negative influence of EMI caused by them.
In the current configuration it was put as a second layer on top of the battery and the PCB additionally protected by a layer of aluminium foil.

Flight controller: connect engines over ESC

ESCs could be powered at least in two ways: directly from PCB and from FC itself over BEC.
Because in this configuration they have been powered from the PCB, it is important to turn off power supply from the BEC. It should be done from removing red wire from the connector which goes to FC.

All of them is connected to MAIN OUT of the board and nuances of doing this right has been described above.

Handmade frame

For the 1st version I decided to go with hadnmade frame: it is R&D project, the configuration is not finalized and frame should support extension and reconfiguration. As an afforable material I decided to use particleboard, but plastic could be even better -- it is lighter, still robust and is also convenient for drilling and sawing.

The main question I should answer first which thikness of frame and the length of axis would support 2kg of thurst on each axis?

To answer this question you have to solve the classical problem of a cantilever beam in bending. ChatGpt helped with this, but I wasn't sure about stiffness coefficient of my material or calculations by itself wasn't fully correct and practice shown the final frame doesn't satisfy full requirements. I solved this issue by made a second frame and fastened it to the first one, the rigidity was now sufficient to withstand the traction with a reserve.

Another key aspect is put center of masses in the center of copter. Otherwise you have to do a black magic on software part to adjust thurst of motors to compensate center of masses somewhere else rather than in the center of copter.

In the case of the 1st version it was easy, because the main battery give ~85% of the weight and putting it in the center was a naturally caused decision. Couping with EMI also narrowed down possible options and each EMI sensitive part was put on a separate layer.

The production ready solution should covered elegant decision within all constraints mentioned above. Significant investments into ergonomic R&D is a task of the 2nd version of copter.
